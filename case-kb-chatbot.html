Case study · 2026 · Prototype

Knowledge base chatbot with citations and a refusal path

A retrieval-augmented question-answering system over my own Indonesian articles. It answers only from the documents, cites the source of every answer, and says "I don't know" when nothing is relevant — instead of guessing. Every returned answer is a verbatim passage with a clickable source, so nothing can be fabricated.

Built by me · Python standard library, no vector database, no paid model API · runs locally, no server

Measured twice: 93.3% top-1 and 10 of 10 refusals in-sample, then 80.0% top-1 and 2 of 5 refusals held-out. Both numbers are below, with the failures named.

The problem

Product and policy answers are inconsistent when the knowledge lives scattered across documents, and the expensive failure is a confident answer that is wrong. A system that answers only from official documents, cites its source, and is willing to say "there is nothing in my documents about that" is safer than one that guesses — so I built the retrieval and the refusal path, and then measured both.

What the system does, and what stays with me

How it runs

corpus articles (suksesdenganemas.com, fetched read-only via REST API)
        ↓
split each article into ~160-word passages  →  tokenize (lowercase, drop Indonesian stopwords)
        ↓
index passages with BM25 (Okapi BM25, k1=1.5, b=0.75 — implemented from scratch)
        ↓
user question  →  tokenize  →  score every passage
        ↓
top score ≥ 4.5  ──►  return top passage VERBATIM + citations (title, URL, score)
top score <  4.5  ──►  "Saya tidak menemukan jawabannya di dokumen yang saya miliki."
        ↓
unanswered questions become a gap list for the knowledge base itself

Two real runs

In scope — "Apa itu spread harga emas dan bagaimana cara menghitungnya?" returned the spread article's own FAQ as the answer ("Apa arti spread harga emas? Selisih antara harga saat membeli dan harga saat menjual kembali emas…") and cited Spread Harga Beli dan Jual Emas: Panduan Lengkap with its URL.

Out of scope — "Bagaimana cara membuat paspor di kantor imigrasi?" returned "Saya tidak menemukan jawabannya di dokumen yang saya miliki." (top score 3.39, below the 4.5 threshold). The system knows it is a gold-investment knowledge base, so it refuses rather than improvise an answer about passports.

Inside this particular set the 4.5 threshold does sit in a gap: out-of-scope questions share at most one or two generic words with the corpus (top score 4.21), while every in-scope question shares many content words (top score 5.25 and up). The two top-1 misses are near-duplicate pairs of my own articles that make the same point in different words, and both were recovered by the top-3 citations. Read those figures as a description of the set the threshold was fitted to, not as a property of the system — the held-out run below is what tests that.

Held-out — 15 questions written after the threshold was frozen

The set was written to the protocol in tests/HELDOUT-PROTOCOL.md: 10 in-scope questions covering 10 different articles, one question per article and none of them a reworded copy of the original 30, plus 5 out-of-scope questions in two labelled kinds — 3 plainly unrelated and 2 gold-adjacent (about gold or personal finance but not answerable from these 12 articles). Commit 543b440 records the set before the score file existed, so the order of events is checkable in the repository history. The threshold was not moved in either direction.

The held-out refusal figure is much worse, and it is the one that counts: 10 out of 10 on the set the threshold was fitted to became 2 out of 5 on questions it had never seen. Retrieval held up (top-3 100%, every in-scope question still answered), but the refusal path did not generalise: the two score classes overlap across the two sets, because the highest out-of-scope score here (6.41) is above the lowest in-sample in-scope score (5.25). A single top-score cut, fitted to one 40-question gap, was never going to separate them in general.

The three questions it answered when it should have refused:

  • Gold-adjacent, 6.405 — Mana yang lebih baik untuk dana pensiun, emas atau reksa dana saham? was answered from Dollar Cost Averaging Emas: Cara Investasi Cerdas. The corpus says nothing at all about mutual funds; the match rides on dana and baik. This is the highest score any out-of-scope question has reached in this project.
  • Gold-adjacent, 4.544 — Berapa harga emas Antam hari ini di Jakarta? was answered from the spot-price article, which quotes one dated snapshot (Rp2.64 million per gram on 6 September 2026). Passing a stale number off as the current price is the single most damaging failure this system can have.
  • Unrelated, 5.218 — Bagaimana cara memilih jurusan kuliah yang sesuai minat? was answered from Emas Digital vs Emas Fisik. It shares only generic words (memilih, sesuai) with the corpus, but BM25 rewards a rare word wherever it lands.

The failure pattern is exactly what the protocol predicted: a question about a completely foreign topic is usually caught (2 of 3 unrelated refused), while a question that speaks the corpus vocabulary but asks for a fact the articles never state is answered with confidence (0 of 2 gold-adjacent refused). That asymmetry is the dangerous one — a reader can tell at a glance that a passport answer is missing, but has to fact-check a price.

Two in-scope questions also lost top-1, both to a neighbouring article that argues the same thing in different words: Kesalahan apa yang biasanya bikin pemula gagal menabung emas? retrieved the DCA article instead of the savings article, and Lebih baik beli emas sekaligus dengan dana besar, atau dicicil rutin setiap bulan? retrieved the emergency-fund article instead of the DCA one. Both correct sources were still inside the top-3 citations.

What was deliberately not done after seeing this result: the threshold was not lowered to rescue the in-scope figures, not raised to rescue the refusals, and the question set was not edited after scoring — any of those would turn the held-out run back into an in-sample one and destroy its only value. The fix belongs in the retriever (for example, requiring the question to overlap the matched passage on a content word that is not a generic connector, or refusing when the top passage answers a different question than the one asked), and it has to be judged on a new held-out set, because this one is now spent.

The benefit claimed on this page remains about not fabricating, not speed: manual answer-finding time was never measured, so no time-saving number appears anywhere.

Limitations

  • Extractive only. It quotes the passage verbatim; it does not write a natural-language answer, so a paraphrased question surfaces the right passage in raw form.
  • Lexical retrieval. BM25 matches words, not meaning — a synonym ("buyback" vs "jual kembali") can miss until both terms appear in the corpus.
  • Small, single-topic corpus. 12 articles on gold, one language (Indonesian). Results do not generalise to other domains.
  • Corpus-specific threshold. 4.5 was calibrated on this corpus's score gap — and on the same test set used to report the results, which makes those figures in-sample. The held-out set now exists and it shows the cut does not generalise (2 of 5 refusals); 4.5 stays a value fitted to one set, not a validated one.
  • No multi-turn memory, no access control, no rate limiting — it is a single-user local demo.
  • One evaluator (me). The gold source per question was assigned by one person with no inter-rater check.
