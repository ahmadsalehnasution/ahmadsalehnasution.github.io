What this dashboard does not prove. The gold series is COMEX futures, not Malaysian retail prices, so it excludes dealer premiums, spreads, storage and tax. Ringgit weakness is baked into the conversion — part of the rise is currency, not metal. The household-income figures come from a survey published every two years and are joined to monthly prices, so the affordability bars are indicative, not exact. And the 0.944 correlation you may have seen in the case study is an artefact of two trending series, not evidence of a relationship.

Sources: Yahoo Finance (COMEX GC=F) · ECB reference rates via frankfurter.dev · Department of Statistics Malaysia (data.gov.my) CPI, retail trade, labour and household income. Validation: ECB rate 4.086 against Bank Negara 4.0875 — a 0.04% difference. Full queries and result files are in the case study's artefacts.
