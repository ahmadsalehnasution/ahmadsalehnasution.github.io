Case study · 2026 · Prototype

Evaluating AI answers in Indonesian

A 30-question Indonesian test set with human-written reference answers, run against two assistant configurations. 60 answers were generated and scored on relevance, factual accuracy, policy compliance and honesty about missing live data.

My own test design and reference answers · agent-generated answers and first-pass scoring · scoring sample of 20 answers awaiting human verification

The problem

A company that puts an Indonesian-language AI assistant in front of customers has no idea how often the answers are wrong, off-topic, or outside policy. Without measurement, "improving the prompt" is guesswork. The job is to turn answer quality into a number that can be tracked — and to be honest about the fact that the standard itself is a human judgement.

What I did, and what the agent did

  • I designed the test. 30 Indonesian questions on gold and personal finance in Malaysia and Indonesia, including six deliberate traps: requests for guaranteed returns, requests for personal investment advice, a request for personal data, and a question carrying a false premise. Every reference answer was written and sourced by hand, with the date of the data and a note where sources conflict.
  • I defined two scoring modes. Price questions cannot be scored on accuracy — the correct answer changes daily — so they are scored on honesty: does the model refuse to invent a live figure and point to a checkable source instead? Rule-based questions are scored on accuracy against my reference answer.
  • The agent generated and pre-scored. 60 answers across two configurations (A = plain assistant, B = the same assistant given explicit compliance rules), then a first-pass score against the rubric, flagged for human review.
  • The agent did not get the last word. A 20-answer sample is scored by me without seeing the AI scores; the agreement rate between the two decides how much of the machine scoring survives.

How it runs

30 Indonesian questions + hand-written reference answers   (human)
        ↓
agent answers each question under configuration A and configuration B
        ↓
agent scores every answer against the rubric  →  relevance / accuracy / compliance / honesty / language
        ↓
human scores a 20-answer sample WITHOUT seeing the AI scores      (human)
        ↓
agreement rate between AI scorer and human scorer  →  decides whether the numbers are publishable
        ↓
failure taxonomy  →  concrete fix recommendations for the prompt

Three findings, including the ones that damage my own hypothesis

  1. The guardrails changed compliance by one answer in thirty. I expected the unguarded configuration to invent a live gold price. It did not: both configurations refused and pointed to sources. The honest reading is that this model is already trained to disclaim missing real-time data, so the added instruction had almost nothing left to fix on that axis.
  2. The one clear invention was not about price. For a question about delivery times — where my reference answer states plainly that no verifiable figure exists — configuration A produced specific delivery windows. The written rule did not prevent it; configuration B produced the same shape of answer. That is a prompt-design problem I have not solved yet.
  3. The largest failure category is the least trustworthy. 10 of the 19 flags are "unnatural Indonesian", which is the scorer's stylistic taste rather than a grounded criterion. This is exactly why the human sample exists, and it is the finding most likely to change once the sample is scored.

These are AI pre-scores, not verified results. The compliance and accuracy figures stay provisional until the 20-answer human sample is complete and the agreement rate is published alongside them.

Limitations

  • The rubric is my own construction, so it is subjective at the margins.
  • The first-pass scoring was done by one AI model using criteria it is also being judged against — a conflict worth naming rather than hiding.
  • Both configurations use the same underlying model and differ only in the system instruction, so nothing here generalises to other models or providers.
  • One human scorer only: there is no inter-rater reliability check.
  • 30 questions is a small sample and cannot stand in for the whole Indonesian-speaking user population.
  • Security testing is out of scope. Jailbreaks and prompt injection are not measured.
  • No commercial outcome is measured. No time saving or revenue effect is claimed.
