Indonesian market brief agent

An agent that produces a weekly brief on the Indonesian gold market where every figure traces to a page it actually fetched. Claims whose sources fail to load are listed as rejected — never quoted from a search snippet — and nothing reaches anyone without human approval.

Built and operated by me · runs on a weekly schedule · drafts only, nothing is published by the agent

The problem

Monitoring the Indonesian gold market by hand means opening BPS, Bank Indonesia, Pegadaian and a few news sites, then reconciling figures that disagree and dates that do not line up. The expensive failure is not missing news — it is publishing a number that is stale or wrong. One out-of-date interest rate in a customer conversation does more damage than a week of missed headlines.

The beneficial claim here is deliberately narrow: error catch rate and traceability, not time saved. Manual preparation time was never measured, so no time-saving figure is claimed anywhere on this page.

The brief also states openly what it could not verify: the latest retail price for the gold product required was not available from a source that could be fetched, so the brief reports the last verified figure with its date instead of quietly quoting an unverified number.

Limitations

  • Scope is fixed to gold, consumer prices, the policy rate and one exchange rate. Tax, zakat and regulation are deliberately out of scope.
  • Two of eight sources failed to fetch, and the failing set will differ day to day — the brief cannot guarantee the freshest retail price.
  • The retail price comes from media citing the official page, not from the official page directly. Labelled secondary, but still second-hand.
  • Derived figures mix data dates (a 1 October retail price against a 12 October spot price) and are flagged as indicative; a strict comparison needs same-day inputs.
  • One human reviewer only, so there is no check on what counts as "verified".
  • The agent cannot detect a source that is itself wrong — it can only confirm that the figure is where the source says it is.
  • No cost control on the number of fetches per run, and no automatic detection when a source changes its URL scheme.
